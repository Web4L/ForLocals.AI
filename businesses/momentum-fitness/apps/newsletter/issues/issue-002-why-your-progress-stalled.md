If your progress has stalled, you've probably been told to do *more*. More weight. More days.
More discipline.

Sometimes that's right. But just as often, a plateau isn't a willpower problem at all — it's a
**movement quality** problem. And no amount of "more" fixes a movement problem. It just makes
it louder.

## The physics of a plateau

Your body is brilliant at one thing: getting the job done with the least possible effort. So
when a movement pattern is inefficient — say your hips don't fully extend, or your shoulder
can't stabilize overhead — your body quietly *cheats* by borrowing from somewhere else.

For a while, that works. You still lift the weight. But you've hit a ceiling, because the
weak link caps how much the whole system can do. Adding more load doesn't raise the ceiling —
it just hammers the weak link until something complains.

The fix isn't more. It's **better**. Restore the pattern, and suddenly the strength you
already had has somewhere to go. People are often shocked at how fast they progress again once
the movement itself is clean.

> One principle, plainly: you don't break a plateau by pushing harder against it. You break it
> by removing the inefficiency that created it.

## Proof

A member came to us frustrated that his bench press hadn't moved in a year. We barely touched
his chest. We spent two weeks teaching his shoulder blades to set and stabilize — the
foundation the press was missing. Within a month the number he'd been stuck at for a *year*
moved. Same muscles. Better movement.

## Try this today

Next time a lift feels stuck, film one set from the side. Don't judge the weight — watch the
*movement*. Is anything shifting, collapsing, or compensating? That's usually your real
ceiling, and it's fixable.
